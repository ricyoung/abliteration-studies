\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study compared direction-based and optimisation-based refusal removal on
an earlier and a later release of four open-weight model families and one
smaller pair, under one search budget, KL cap and selection rule, with
held-out refusal scored by an independent judge. Refusal removal did not
become uniformly harder. After direction-based removal the generation effect
was large for OLMo and the small pair, small for Qwen and Gemma and near zero
for Mistral Small;
beyond the newer originals' higher starting refusal, the pooled effect was
near zero with a pair-level interval that spans large effects of either sign.
ARA lost less effectiveness than the direction-based methods in two families
and more in one. At the tool's 100-token window, keyword counts matched judged
refusal on average but not model by model, and first-token KL did not bound
capability loss. Claims that a model generation resists refusal removal, or
that a removal tool succeeds, therefore require several model pairs analysed
as pairs, the unedited models as reference, several method classes and
optimisation seeds, held-out judged evaluation and a full-sequence measure of
collateral change.

Four directions follow. First, a second optimisation seed for the 32
main-study cells would test whether the family-specific effects exceed
run-to-run variation. Second, running heretic-org's reported Qwen3.8-27B ARA
trial through this pipeline, and refitting the ARA exports on the search
hardware, would show whether the Qwen reversal and the Gemma-4-31B result
depend on the search or on the release step. Third, a human-labelled audit of
the responses on which keyword matching and the judges disagree would
establish which measure is correct and estimate judge error on edited models
\cite{gao2026how}. Fourth, judge labels for non-selected trials would allow
Pareto fronts to be re-selected with a judged objective and a full-sequence
divergence constraint. Until such checks are routine, a model's apparent
resistance to refusal removal should be read as a statement about one family,
one method and one measure, not about its generation.
