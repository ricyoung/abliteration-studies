\noindent Abliteration tools remove refusal from open-weight language models by editing weights, and the released
model is chosen from a search by a selection rule that caps its divergence from the original. Tools are usually
compared with keyword counts on tuning prompts, without reporting that rule.
This study rebuilt archived Heretic~1.x models with general- and code-derived directions for six Qwen2.5
models and evaluated them, alongside Heretic~2.0, two external tools run once at their documented settings and two
community releases, on 972 held-out harmful prompts scored by a safety judge and five capability benchmarks; each
configuration contributed one search per base model. At the archived dev-KL cap of 0.01, the twelve 1.x
models removed between 0.9 and 73.1 percentage points (pp) of refusal. Heretic~2.0 DiM, selected under a ten-fold
looser cap, left 25.9~pp less residual refusal than the same edit family released by 1.x on Qwen2.5-32B
and 19.0~pp less on Qwen2.5-7B; both were significant for these checkpoints but within search-to-search
variation. In a post hoc re-export of one Qwen2.5-32B 1.x search at the 2.0 cap, residual
refusal fell by 61.7~pp [58.5, 64.7], to 35.8~pp below 2.0 DiM, a reversal also within
search-to-search variation; trial budget, seed, search range, objective, system prompt and marker list still differed,
and the re-exported model had diverged about twice as far as 2.0 DiM. Comparisons of abliteration tools should report the
selection rule and the realised divergence of the released model. In one search on one model, changing only the
selection cap moved residual refusal by more than the version contrast.
