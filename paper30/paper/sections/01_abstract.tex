\noindent Abliteration tools remove refusal from open-weight large language models (LLMs) by weight editing, and the
released model is chosen from a search by a selection rule that caps its divergence from the original. Tools are usually
compared with keyword refusal counts on tuning prompts, without reporting that rule.
This study rebuilt archived Heretic~1.x models with general- and code-derived refusal directions for six Qwen2.5
models (Qwen2.5-7B, -14B and -32B and their Qwen2.5-Coder variants) and evaluated them, alongside the Heretic~2.0 methods
difference-in-means (DiM), MPOA and Arbitrary-Rank Ablation, two external tools run once at their documented settings (OBLITERATUS and jwest33's
abliterator) and two community abliterated releases, on 972 held-out harmful prompts (JailbreakBench, XSTest,
StrongREJECT, HarmBench and malicious-code requests) scored by the automatic WildGuard judge without human audit, and on
MMLU-Pro, GSM8K, IFEval, HumanEval and MBPP; each configuration contributed one search per base model. At the archived
dev-KL cap of 0.01, the twelve 1.x models removed 0.9 to 73.1 percentage points (pp) of refusal. Heretic~2.0 DiM,
selected under a ten-fold looser cap, left 25.9~pp less residual refusal than 1.x on Qwen2.5-32B and 19.0~pp less on
Qwen2.5-7B, significant for these checkpoints but within search-to-search variation. In a post hoc re-export of one
Qwen2.5-32B 1.x search at the 2.0 cap, residual refusal fell by 61.7~pp, reversing the version contrast, again within
search-to-search variation; other search settings still differed, and the re-exported model had diverged about twice
as far as 2.0 DiM. Comparisons of abliteration tools should report the selection rule and the realised divergence of the
released model. In one search on one model, changing only the selection cap moved residual refusal by more than the
version contrast.
