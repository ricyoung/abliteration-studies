\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Judged on held-out prompts, archived Heretic~1.x models removed up to 73.1~pp of refusal from six Qwen2.5 models,
depending on the model and the direction source, without a detectable capability cost. Under the archived settings,
the same edit released by Heretic~2.0 left less residual refusal than when released by 1.x on both base models
tested, 25.9~pp less on Qwen2.5-32B and 19.0~pp less on Qwen2.5-7B. Released from the same Qwen2.5-32B search at the
2.0 cap, however, the 1.x model left 11.6\% of the harmful prompts refused, 35.8~pp less than 2.0 DiM though more
than 2.0 MPOA and ARA, so on that model the version gap measured under the archived settings reflected the
selection cap rather than the version. External tools run at their documented settings ranged from no change in
refusal (OBLITERATUS \texttt{basic}) to near-complete removal (jwest33's abliterator as released on Qwen3.5-4B, 1.0\%), and one
architecture-handling detail changed jwest33's result on Qwen3.5-4B by 34.8~pp. A code-derived refusal direction
gave no consistent advantage on malicious-code prompts, and keyword counts differed from judged refusal after strong
edits but rarely changed the order of tools. Comparisons of abliteration tools and versions should therefore report
the selection rule and the divergence of the released model, use a judge on held-out prompts, and compare tools at
matched selection caps.
